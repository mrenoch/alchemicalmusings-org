This year Earth lost two of my favorite aliens - Mork, played by Robin Williams in *Mork and Mindy* and Spock, played by Leonard Nemoy in *Star Trek*. Mork and Spock were both ambassadors to humans, living among us and illuminating our socio-cultural quirks through our encounters with their radical otherness. Mork’s surreal and naive silliness and Spock’s emotionless logic helped reveal ourselves through their extra-terrestrial eyes.
“Nanu, Nanu” is the traditional Orkan salutation that Mork used whenever he greeted someone, and it was accompanied by a gesture that was inspired by Nemoy’s Vulcan salute
Williams is on the record saying that his inspiration for the Orkan greeting was the Spock’s salute. And Nemoy, who grew up in an Orthodox Jewish family, has talked about the origins of the salute in the priestly blessing:
To this day, the Jewish liturgy includes a part of the service when the priests, or Kohanim, stand in front of the congregation and bless them.  While they priests bless the congregation they make a specific gesture with their hands, a gesture that is often inscribed on gravestones of priests. Traditionally, the congregants are forbidden from peeking at the priests while the are being blessed, but Nemoy has described peeking as a child and using the gesture he discovered for the Vulcan Salute.
[![800px-Grave_Rabbi_Meschullam_Kohn](/images/2015/03/800px-Grave_Rabbi_Meschullam_Kohn-300x225.jpg)](/images/2015/03/800px-Grave_Rabbi_Meschullam_Kohn.jpg)
The [priestly blessing](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priestly_Blessing) is short and sweet:
> May the Lord bless you and guard you
> May the Lord make their face shed light upon you and be gracious unto you
> May the Lord lift up their face unto you and give you peace [Shalom]

 
An interesting aspect of this hand gesture is it’s literal connection to it’s source. The gesture resembles the Hebrew letter “Shin”, a three-headed letter that evokes a flame. Many words begin with the letter Shin, but in the context of the priestly blessing one of the primary associations is the Hebrew word Shalom, the last word of the blessing and it’s thematic core. Shalom means hello, goodbye and peace. Nanu Nanu. Live long and prosper.

[![peace_crowd](/images/2015/03/peace_crowd-300x169.jpg)](/images/2015/03/peace_crowd.jpg)Semantically, I think this gesture is also fascinating. Pointing at someone, with an arrow, spear, gun or finger is implicitly an act of aggression. Perhaps a universal sign of aggression. By implication, raising your hands and showing your palms is a universal sign of surrender. Hands up, don’t shoot - I come in peace.
When Colonial explorers/pirates disembarked from their ships, they most likely communicated with this universal gesture of surrender -- even if they actively deceived and later betrayed the people they encountered. Similarly, fictional encounters with peaceful alien species begin with this familiar exchange.
The question of how to talk to an extra-terrestrial is a serious theoretical and practical one, whether or not we ever make contact. There are many insights to learn from the thought experiment of encountering an extra-terrestrial, as Stan Tenen from the Meru Foundation takes up in his essay *[The Shape of Information:](http://www.meru.org/Noetic/ShapeofInfoA3Higraf.pdf) How to talk to an extra-terrestrial*. SETI, the Search for Extra-Terestrial Intelligence employs the anthropologist Douglas Vakoch as their full time [director of interstellar message composition](http://www.newyorker.com/books/joshua-rothman/the-man-who-speaks-for-earth), who is responsible for designing the messages we send to other civilizations.
In this context, recent charges against police officers Peter Liang, who killed the unarmed Akai Gurley in a stairwell, are especially damning. Laing was patrolling the stairwell with his gun drawn - an implicitly aggressive gesture. When a gun is drawn for any other reason than self-defense, the policeman ceases operating as an Officer of the Peace. They become an Officer of War. Pre-emptive strikes are also exceedingly problematic - almost like arguing that you hit someone back first.
The Ferguson protests, and the growing #blacklivesmatters campaign has taken up Michael Brown’s surrender as one of their rallying cries. Hands up, don’t shoot. This gesture represents the universal sign for peaceful greetings, and speaks directly to the overt aggression and hostility of the Officers of War around the country. Holster your weapon. Stop pointing at me. Live long and prosper.
