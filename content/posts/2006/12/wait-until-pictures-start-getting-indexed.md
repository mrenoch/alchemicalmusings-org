![police_sketch.jpg](/images/2006/12/police_sketch.thumbnail.jpg)Well, I called it:
In in [class I took](http://moglen.law.columbia.edu/CPC/) with Eben Moglen I predicted in a class discussion that pictures on the internet would soon be indexed:
[Re: video cameras](http://old.law.columbia.edu/CPC/discuss/21.html) (Feb. 11, 2005)
Many people in the class were [skeptical](http://old.law.columbia.edu/CPC/discuss/18.html)...
Well, here it is, less than two years later:
 [Face Search Engine Raises Privacy Concerns](http://yro.slashdot.org/article.pl?sid=06/12/19/1923259)
Of course, there are standard objections to the two primary critiques of surviellance "paranioa".

1. If I am not breaking the law, why should I care?
2. There is so much informatoin being gathered, who could possibly sort through it all?

The responses to these objections should be well rehersed.

1. Pervasive Omniscient Surviellance [will have an impact](http://alchemicalmusings.org/2006/04/01/permanent-records/) on the basic fabric of personal social and cultural interactions as we currently understand them.
2. The [AI is coming](http://alchemicalmusings.org/2005/09/25/is-anyone-watching-grandma/), getting more powerful by the day.
